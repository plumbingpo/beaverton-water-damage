Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Beaverton

Wood floors react to water quickly. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. This takes longer than drying carpet or drywall, often a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Signs to Look For

  • Planks pulling loose from the subfloor or peaking where they meet
  • Finish turning white, cloudy or peeling in the wet area
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
  • Puffy baseboards and casings next to the affected boards
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
Our Process

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Moisture

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Drying Too Fast

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Damage Beyond the Room

Water under a floor travels. Cabinets and wall plates do not stop it, so it may enter adjacent rooms or come out at the ceiling one floor down.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. Finish may turn cloudy. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.
