Every water damage restoration job starts with extraction, and no other early step matters more. The faster standing water is removed from your property, the less damage occurs to structural materials, flooring, personal belongings and building systems. Every hour that water sits on surfaces, it penetrates deeper into materials, soaking through carpet into padding, wicking up drywall, saturating wood framing and seeping into subflooring. Across Beaverton, Beaverton Restoration handles emergency extraction with industrial portable and truck-mounted machines that can remove hundreds of gallons a minute.

Few water removal systems match the power of a truck-mounted extractor, which draws water from structural materials, carpeting and hard flooring far better than rental or consumer machines. Where the truck hoses will not reach, we bring in submersible pumps, strong portable extractors and specialty tools made for water caught between floor layers, inside wall cavities or in tight spaces.

Removing visible water is only part of what professional extraction involves. Water migrates behind baseboards, below flooring, into wall cavities and under cabinets, and our technicians, who are IICRC-certified, locate it with moisture detection tools. Before drying starts, we use specialty tools to pull as much moisture as we can out of those hidden spots, so structural drying takes far less time and equipment and secondary mold is less likely.

How to Tell You Need Water Extraction

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water accumulating after a fire suppression system discharges
  • Saturated carpet that squishes underfoot or shows water pooling
  • Water entering through a flooded basement stairwell or window well
  • Active leaks from plumbing, appliances or HVAC systems
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Ceilings, light fixtures or walls that are actively leaking water
  • Storm water collecting in the lowest level of the home

Water Extraction Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Store a bucket and spare towels where you can grab them, along with the number for a restoration company
  • Early leak signs show up near appliances, toilets and sink cabinets, so check those spots on a schedule
  • Place leak detection sensors in the key risk areas and test that they work
  • Pour water into the sump pit a few times a year to confirm the pump kicks on
  • Check the supply hoses on your appliances and swap out any that look old or worn
  • Program (562) 647-3735 into your phone so you can call immediately in an emergency
